SECTION A — PROTOCOL COMPARISON: MQTT vs CoAP (10 Questions)
==============================================================================

Q1. What transport protocol does MQTT use? What transport protocol does CoAP
    use? What is the fundamental difference between the two?

    Answer: MQTT runs on TCP, while CoAP runs on UDP. The main difference comes down to reliability versus lightness. TCP (MQTT) establishes a strict connection first, making sure every packet arrives in order and without errors—it’s built for stability. UDP (CoAP), on the other hand, just blasts packets out as fast as it can without checking if the receiver is ready or if packets got dropped along the way. It’s way leaner but leaves reliability up to the application.

Q2. In today's session, MQTT used a publish/subscribe model and CoAP used a
    request/response model. Explain the difference between these two models
    in your own words using the smart home scenario as an example.

    Answer: • Publish/Subscribe (MQTT): Think of this as a middleman system. A smart light switch doesn't talk directly to a bulb. Instead, the switch publishes a message saying "light is ON" to a central broker under a topic like livingroom/light. The bulb just sits there listening (subscribed) to that topic. Neither device needs to know where the other lives on the network.
	   • Request/Response (CoAP): This is a direct conversation, just like visiting a website. Your phone (client) talks directly to the IP address of the light bulb (server) and sends a command: "Hey, update your status to ON." The bulb processes it and replies directly to the phone with an "Okay, done."

Q4. In your L3 MQTT publisher, the light relay topic used retain=True.
    CoAP does not have a retain flag. How does CoAP handle the same problem
    of "what is the current state of the light?"

    Answer: MQTT uses retain=True so a central broker remembers the last message for anyone who joins late. Since CoAP doesn't use a broker, it doesn't need a retain flag at all. If you want to know if a light is on, your app simply sends a direct CoAP GET request to the bulb and the bulb replies with its current status on the spot.

Q5. MQTT uses TLS for security. CoAP uses DTLS. Why can't CoAP use TLS?
    What is the key difference between TLS and DTLS?

    Answer: TLS was designed specifically for TCP, so it expects a stable, ordered stream of data. Because CoAP runs over UDP—where packets can arrive out of order or get lost entirely—standard TLS completely falls apart during the encryption handshake. That’s why CoAP uses DTLS (Datagram TLS). It’s tweaked specifically to handle lost or reordered UDP packets during the handshake so you still get full encryption without needing TCP.

Q9. The CoAP client measured latency by recording time before and after each
    GET request. List TWO factors that could increase round-trip time in a
    real deployment (not running on localhost).

    Answer: While loopback latency in a lab is fast, two real-world factors drag it down:
		1. Network Congestion & Wi-Fi Interference: Dead spots, thick walls, or too many devices on 2.4GHz cause UDP packet loss, forcing CoAP to retry sending messages, which spikes latency.
		2. Device Processing Lag: Tiny microcontrollers (like an ESP8266) take extra CPU cycles to decrypt DTLS packets or process hardware inputs before sending a response back.
